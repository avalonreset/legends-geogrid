# Client report acceptance policy

A release must preserve its approved visual contract across businesses. Passing geometry tests alone does not establish visual quality or real-business readiness.

## Editorial and visual contract

Follow [the versioned design contract](REPORT_DESIGN.md) for both report stages
and HTML/PDF output modes. Dark page backgrounds are pure black `#000000`.

- Use restrained dark backgrounds, white primary copy and muted secondary copy. Reserve green, yellow and red for rank categories. Do not color every heading or statistic.
- Align titles, captions, prose and maps to the same editorial column. Keep service findings and their map on one page when readable; shorten redundant explanation before shrinking type.
- Prioritize a large, geographically registered street map. A schematic fixture proves layout mechanics, not real-client cartographic acceptance.
- Use compact legends, readable small pins and subtle alternating neutral distance bands. Distance guides are not service boundaries or claims of continuous ranking coverage.
- Fit the map to useful measured locations with deliberate padding. Preserve a full-extent view when the main map excludes sampled points. Never silently crop evidence, circles or attribution.
- Preserve source aspect ratio and geographic registration. Verify effective resolution at printed size and inspect the final PDF at reading size.
- Report copy must contain no em dash characters. Prefer a period, comma, colon or parentheses appropriate to the sentence. Reject or normalize supplied copy before rendering and verify extracted PDF text.

## Business identity and evidence

A domain starts research; it does not establish a business location. Resolve the business profile, exact target identifiers and a defensible center before presenting business-centered measurements. Conflicting historical directories require reconciliation. A service city is not automatically the headquarters. If identity remains unresolved, request the missing profile link and continue independent preparation without inventing a business pin.

An explicitly commissioned market-centered study may use a labeled geographic reference point. It must not label that point as the business location or imply that multiple service markets share one verified profile.

Preserve acquisition provenance, observed result depth and distinct missing-data states. Do not interpret short result lists as full-depth negative evidence. Show bounded stopping honestly; a collection limit is not a discovered visibility boundary.

## Search-setting calibration

### Study purpose and market fit

Before a dense paid scan, state the business decision the study will change. Separate the business's advertised cities, verified operating base, practical service territory and proposed search origins. A published city page is not sufficient evidence that a market is commercially serviceable. If operating constraints are unknown, label candidate markets as provisional and perform bounded discovery first.

Discovery should check both Maps and organic website visibility where both could generate enquiries. Do not describe a Maps-only absence as general search invisibility. Compare representative service queries, verify that returned competitors are relevant, and keep single-point probes distinct from full-area studies. A mobile event business may benefit more immediately from organic landing-page and lead-qualification work than a dense Maps report.

Authorize expansion based on a defined coverage question, a positive foothold, or a relevant competitor comparison. Repeating hundreds of equivalent negative observations is not automatically useful. Do not choose a business's operating territory from rankings alone; travel, job value, capacity and actual enquiries require separate evidence. Visual and technical acceptance do not substitute for this market-fit gate.

Before broad paid collection, verify exact identity with a branded query and calibrate representative service queries at the proposed market reference. Record provider zoom, location method, device, language and search_this_area explicitly. A service-area profile may behave differently under a restrictive map viewport; do not equate an omitted result with profile nonexistence or suspension.

If the branded control conflicts with a directly verified profile, run a bounded identical-query comparison that changes one setting at a time. Keep those controls separate from service-query denominators. A branded-query result does not establish nonbrand service visibility. Test representative nonbrand queries before selecting the study mode.

Choose and label the mode based on the actual question: restrictive viewport discovery and broader coordinate-centered search are different measurements. Never change settings midway or pool unlike observations. If a different mode is needed, create a separate run and retain the original. Missing or short lists remain unknown beyond their observed depth under either mode.

## Release acceptance

1. Run synthetic fixtures and meaningful regression tests.
2. Process a second real business through the shared configuration and collection path, without client-specific renderer branches.
3. Compare its actual PDF with the approved visual reference, including every map page, longest headings, legends, distance bands and page breaks.
4. Review factual claims and recommended actions against retained evidence.
5. Deliver the inspected PDF. Record unresolved limitations explicitly. Reopen acceptance when a user identifies material visual drift.

Synthetic examples and automated tests are necessary evidence, but cannot substitute for the real-business acceptance step.
